:::glance
- **Deal:** an Eldridge-led consortium with the US DFC signed on 25 Nov 2026 to swap about $10B of Lukoil securities for Lukoil International, a portfolio put at about $16B.
- **Already gone:** West Qurna-2 (75%, about 460,000 b/d gross) is moving to Basra Oil Company and Chevron.
- **Still open:** Kazakh stakes under state pre-emption, the Block 10 (Eridu) perimeter, and the Petrotel refinery in insolvency.
- **Gate:** OFAC FAQ 1224 requires complete severance from Lukoil and blocked proceeds; the negotiation licence GL 131L expires on 19 Dec 2026.
- **Earliest closing:** late 2027, with at least one licence renewal needed inside the window.
:::

On 25 November, Todd Boehly's Eldridge [signed a deal with Lukoil](https://live.euronext.com/en/financial-news/exclusive-boehly-signs-us-backed-share-swap-deal-buy-lukoil-global-assets) (Reuters) for a consortium that includes the US International Development Finance Corporation (DFC), [UAE-based Catena Investment Holding and Xtellus Partners](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/energy/2026/11/25/todd-boehly-lukoil/). The structure is a swap: Lukoil securities worth $10 billion, frozen in Western funds since 2022, exchanged for Lukoil International GmbH (LIG). Two people familiar with the terms put the portfolio at about $16 billion: operations in 11 countries producing around 350,000 barrels per day.

The headlines read this as a takeover. From the project side, it reads differently. **What was signed is the right to inherit a portfolio whose interfaces were built around a Russian parent, under a sanctions clock, with part of the asset base already gone.**

That is a carve-out problem. And carve-outs fail on interfaces, not on reserves.

## Three numbers the headline does not show

The first number is the gap between $16 billion and the [$22 billion book value](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/russias-lukoil-reaches-tentative-deal-203301323.html) Lukoil itself put on its international business. The bases differ and the gap is not itemised in public. But part of it has a name.

The second number is 460,000. That is roughly how many barrels per day West Qurna-2 produces gross, and it has already left. Under an [OFAC permit dated 31 March 2026](https://interfax.com/newsroom/top-stories/119452/), Lukoil's 75% stake could be transferred to Basra Oil Company, and on 3 November Iraq said terms with Chevron [were agreed](https://worldoil.com/news/2026/11/3/iraq-says-terms-agreed-with-chevron-on-west-qurna-2-contract-signing-targeted-before-year-end/), with signing targeted before year-end. One Iraqi field produced more gross barrels than the entire portfolio now being sold.

The third number is about 19 cents. Reuters reported that the swap securities were [bought from Western funds](https://kurs.com.ua/en/novost/1486880-amerikanskii-miljarder-skupovue-zamorozheni-akciyi-zaradi-aktiviv-lukoilu-reuters) at about 30 cents on the dollar. If that applies to the full $10 billion, the cash behind a $16 billion portfolio is around $3 billion. That spread is not a bargain. It is the budget for every interface that still has to be rebuilt.

## The perimeter is still moving

A portfolio map is not a legal perimeter. Three items decide what the consortium actually inherits.

**Kazakhstan.** Reuters lists major Kazakh oilfields in the package. But the January Carlyle deal [explicitly excluded](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/crude-oil/012926-lukoil-agrees-to-sell-most-international-assets-to-carlyle) Lukoil's Kazakh assets, and Astana has notified OFAC of its [pre-emptive right](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2026/03/10/kazakhstan-tells-us-it-has-priority-in-local-lukoil-buyout-bid) over the 13.5% in Karachaganak, 5% in Tengiz and 12.5% in CPC. Since then, KazMunayGas has said it is [negotiating directly with Lukoil](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2026/08/01/kazakhstan-tells-us-it-has-priority-in-local-lukoil-buyout-bid) on these stakes. And the Karachaganak share [sits in a separate legal entity](https://en.orda.kz/us-sanctions-hit-lukoil-what-will-happen-to-its-assets-in-kazakhstan-9122/), not automatically inside LIG. That is the question I would put first in any data room. As I argued in the [Kazakhstan licensing article](https://evgenymaradin.com/insights/kazakhstan/), CPC export capacity is the live constraint for Kazakh crude.

**Block 10.** Lukoil holds [80% of Block 10](https://interfax.com/newsroom/top-stories/114169/), home to Eridu, the 12.9-billion-barrel discovery I covered in the [West Qurna-2 / Eridu article](https://evgenymaradin.com/insights/eridu/). It produces nothing yet. Whether it sits inside the LIG perimeter is still not public. If it does, the consortium inherits the largest undeveloped option in the portfolio, and any onward sale of it needs separate OFAC review.

**Petrotel.** Romania's refinery has been [out of operation for over a year](https://www.industrialinfo.com/news/article/petrotel-lukoil-restarting-romanian-refinery-shut-by-sanctions--364863), entered [insolvency in August](https://romania-insider.com/lukoil-refinery-romania-insolvency-august-2026), and was [designed to run Urals](https://www.romania-insider.com/index%2ephp/ro-operate-lukoil-refinery-apr-2026). A refinery built around one crude slate does not change supplier by changing owner.

## What a buyer actually receives on day one

In brownfield work, the asset you study is rarely the asset you inherit. The drawings show the plant. The plant runs on interfaces that are not on the drawings: who supplies the feed, who buys the product, who holds the operating licence, who maintains the control system, who signs off the shutdown.

LIG was built as a subsidiary of a vertically integrated Russian major. In my reading, the interfaces a new owner has to rebuild fall into five groups: operatorship, crude slate, parent services, host-government consent and licence continuity.

![Five interfaces the Russian parent used to cover: operatorship, crude slate, parent services, host-government consent, licence continuity](/img/insights/lukoil-international/03.webp)

One of them deserves more than a table row. In a normal carve-out, the seller keeps running trading, IT and technical support under transitional service agreements for 12 to 24 months while the buyer builds its own. **Here, OFAC's first condition is that the sale must completely sever LIG's ties with Lukoil.** Read literally, that removes the usual runway. The buyer may have to stand up its own services on day one, or find a third party who can. That is my reading of the guidance, not a disclosed term of the deal, and it is the item I would cost first.

None of the five is a reservoir question. All of them decide what the portfolio is worth after closing.

## The binding constraint has migrated

I use one formula for every investment case: **Realisable Project Value = Economic Potential × Delivery Certainty.**

The $16 billion is Economic Potential, and Boehly himself says it [moves with oil prices](https://live.euronext.com/en/financial-news/exclusive-boehly-signs-us-backed-share-swap-deal-buy-lukoil-global-assets). Delivery Certainty is set by something else. In a normal acquisition, the binding constraint is the reservoir and the plant. In this one, it has migrated: from reservoir, to licence, to host government, to the interfaces between assets that used to share a parent.

That migration is the same pattern I see in brownfield development. Debottleneck the wells and the constraint moves to separation. Fix separation and it moves to water handling. The engineering value is in seeing where it lands next, before the money is committed.

## A realistic timeline

Two buyers have already tried. Gunvor [withdrew on 6 November 2025](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/refined-products/111425-us-extends-deadline-for-russias-lukoil-to-sell-international-assets) after Treasury said it would block the deal. Carlyle's agreement [expired at the end of July](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/crude-oil/100926-carlyle-ends-active-pursuit-of-lukoils-international-oil-assets) without OFAC approval. Neither failed on asset quality.

The road ahead has four dates that matter:

- **19 Dec 2026:** negotiation licence GL 131L expires. It never authorised the transfer itself; closing needs a separate OFAC authorisation.
- **24 Apr 2027:** current [general licences](https://www.bta.bg/en/news/economy/1225883-us-extends-authorization-for-transactions-with-lukoil-s-foreign-subsidiaries-unt) for retail and the Bulgarian entities expire.
- **Late 2027:** earliest realistic closing on a full-length review, since closing [could take up to a year](https://live.euronext.com/en/financial-news/exclusive-boehly-signs-us-backed-share-swap-deal-buy-lukoil-global-assets).
- **Post-closing, 12 to 24 months:** carve-out and stabilisation. This is my estimate for a portfolio of this breadth, not a disclosed plan.

Between the licence expiry and the earliest closing sits a gap of about seven months. At least one more renewal has to happen inside the closing window, and every renewal is a decision point the consortium does not control.

![Lukoil International timeline: Gunvor and Carlyle attempts, signing on 25 Nov 2026, licence windows, a seven-month licence gap, closing in late 2027 and carve-out](/img/insights/lukoil-international/05.webp)

After stabilisation comes portfolio rationalisation. Back in December 2025, the parties behind the swap idea [indicated](https://www.oilandgas360.com/?p=1041950) they would seek to sell the overseas assets on, likely to energy companies. That matters for engineers. A financial consortium is the bridge owner. The real concept and investment decisions on these refineries, fields and terminals will be made by whoever buys the pieces next, and they will price in every unresolved interface.

## What can still break it

Six days before signing, OFAC [updated FAQ 1224](https://ofac.treasury.gov/faqs/1224) and set the bar in writing. A sale must completely sever LIG's ties with Lukoil. Lukoil must get no windfall, explicitly including through asset or share swaps. Funds owed to Lukoil must stay blocked in an account subject to US jurisdiction. The new owner needs OFAC review before selling material assets on.

Set that against the deal. The consideration is a share swap. The escrow sits in the UAE. Whether that combination meets the test is not established by anything public.

![OFAC FAQ 1224 conditions against the public record of the Eldridge-DFC deal: severance open, share swap to be tested, UAE escrow not established](/img/insights/lukoil-international/06.webp)

Treasury [first rejected the swap idea](https://live.euronext.com/en/financial-news/exclusive-boehly-signs-us-backed-share-swap-deal-buy-lukoil-global-assets) before the State Department backed it. Democrats take control of Congress next year, and [CSIS expects](https://www.csis.org/analysis/us-governments-deal-buy-lukoils-assets-explained) critics to scrutinise the structure and the eventual payout to Lukoil from the escrow. The consortium itself has already changed: IHC and the Al-Khayyat brothers [left a week before signing](https://live.euronext.com/en/financial-news/exclusive-boehly-signs-us-backed-share-swap-deal-buy-lukoil-global-assets).

Then there are the host governments. Each one can turn a portfolio transfer into a country-by-country negotiation.

## The pattern

The third attempt solved the payment problem with a share swap and a UAE escrow. It has not yet solved the interface problem, because that only starts at closing.

This is the same discipline FEL is meant to enforce on a single project. Before FEED, you ask whether the concept is ready to execute, not only whether it is attractive. Before closing a carve-out, you ask which interfaces exist only because of the previous owner, and what it costs to rebuild each one without the previous owner's help.

**A signature fixes the price. The interfaces fix the value.**

Practitioners who have been through a carve-out or a change of operator: which interface broke first in your case, feedstock, licences or people?
