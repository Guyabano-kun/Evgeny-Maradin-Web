Middle East crude output fell from 28.7 mb/d in February to 15.7 mb/d in May 2026. EIA's estimates put it at about 24.7 mb/d in October, still about 14% below February. Reported data runs to July; August to October are Short-Term Energy Outlook estimates, so these recovery figures will move as reported numbers arrive.

The low point still explains the shape. Producers with a route around Hormuz kept most of their output (Saudi Arabia about 62% of its February level, the UAE about 66%), while those without one kept far less (Iraq about 30%, Kuwait about 23%, Qatar and Bahrain under 20%). Oman, which loads outside the Strait, produced more than before.

By October the estimates show Iraq back at its February level and the UAE above it (about 110%), Saudi Arabia at about 88%, Kuwait about 76% and Bahrain about 73%, while Qatar is still at about 41%. Iran is the exception: about 56% of February and lower than in May. Restart time is not the same as recovered deliverability.

For FEL work the point stands: nameplate capacity is not exportable production, and the route to market belongs in the concept, not in the risk register.
