Middle East crude output fell from 28.7 mb/d in February to 15.8 mb/d in May 2026, and by September had recovered to about 21.8 mb/d, still a quarter below its pre-crisis level. The reservoirs did not change; the export routes did. At the low point, producers with a pipeline around Hormuz kept most of their output (Saudi Arabia via Yanbu about 62%, the UAE via Fujairah about 65%), while those without a bypass kept far less (Iraq about 30%, Kuwait about 20%, Qatar and Bahrain under 20%). Oman, which loads outside the Strait, produced more than before.

The recovery is uneven. By September the UAE was back to about 98% of its February level and Iraq to about 84%, but Kuwait was at about 61% and Qatar at about 41%, and Iran was still falling. Restart time is not the same as recovered deliverability.

For FEL work this is the point: nameplate capacity is not exportable production, and the route to market belongs in the concept, not in the risk register.
