What a concept study says - and what it usually means

23 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · Evgeny Maradin

A short translation guide for anyone who signs off on one.

"Minor modifications to existing facilities"
= Nobody has walked the site yet.

"Spare capacity is available"
= On the nameplate. Ask what the plant actually did last summer.

"To be confirmed in FEED"
= Nobody owns this yet.

"Tie-in during the planned shutdown"
= Someone else's shutdown, on a date nobody has agreed.

"Produced water handled by the existing system"
= Until the water rate doubles. Check year 5, not year 1.

"Operations have been consulted"
= One meeting, some slides, no minutes.

Most of us have written a few of these. None of them is wrong. Each one is a question that was postponed.

The cheapest moment to ask it is before FEED, when changing the answer costs a revised report, not a revised contract. That is what a good pre-FEED review is for: turning each phrase back into a question with an owner and a date.

The one I hear most is "to be confirmed in FEED." As a label it is fine. It becomes a problem when it sits next to anything that drives CAPEX: power supply, water disposal, tie-in windows.

Working on a concept, Pre-FEED or a tender pack? A short call is often enough to see where the gaps are.
