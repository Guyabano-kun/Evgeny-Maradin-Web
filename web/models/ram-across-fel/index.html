    Project basis inputs

    Compression CAPEX per train follows the six-tenths rule: train cost = CAPEX(100%) x size^0.6. A train built later is counted at its present value. Deferment is valued as revenue lost in the year it occurs; volume recovered after field life can be credited with the last input.

    Design margin over plateau. No API, ISO or NORSOK standard fixes one plant-level margin over plateau. NORSOK P-100 (cl. 4.2.4) makes the design margin and turndown the project owner's responsibility, and ISO 20815 with ISO 15663 expect the choice to be justified by production assurance and lifecycle cost. Equipment standards give the anchors: NORSOK P-002 sets at least 10% over design rates for scrubbers (P-100 cl. 5.3.2: 20% for process-control fluctuations); API 675 rates metering pumps at no less than 110% of maximum specified flow; API 617 sizes compressor drivers at least 10% above maximum absorbed power; API 610 leaves the rated-to-normal flow margin to the purchaser. Process licensors commonly add 10% on flow, and stacked margins are a known cause of oversized compressors (Kurz, Thorp, Zentmyer; Solar Turbines and Saudi Aramco). Default here: 10%.

    Train count and size. NORSOK P-100 classes each system by the consequence of its failure for deliveries: A, shutdown or reduced production for more than 2 hours; B, less than 2 hours; C, no effect. Gas compression is class A, "vital for production". For some class A services, such as export pumps, the standard requires a spare unit or spare capacity; for compression it leaves the sparing to the project and does not choose between 2 x 100%, 3 x 50% or 2 x 60%: that choice is the regularity and lifecycle-cost optimisation this page runs (ISO 20815, ISO 15663). The configuration table shows which layouts give full N+1 at the chosen margin and the train size that gives full N+1.

    Check C. Major event: loss of one train area for months

    Random failures last hours. Fire, explosion or external damage lasts months and takes out an area. The event removes one train area (the whole plant if there is one train). Repair cost is excluded. An event reaching several trains or a shared utility is worse than shown.

    Check D. Phasing: build the last train later

    Change the deferral in Facility at a glance. Phasing pays when the deferred train adds capacity the field may not need yet; it costs when the deferred train is the N+1 reserve.
