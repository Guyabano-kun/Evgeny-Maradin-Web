How C_inj earns the oil - conformance, not viscosity (light oil)

This is a light-oil carbonate (Thamama-type, μ_oil ~0.3 cP). The water-oil mobility ratio is already favourable - polymer is not here to thicken water to match oil. It diverts injection out of swept hi-K streaks (100-1000 mD) into unswept low-K matrix (1-10 mD). Incremental oil scales with heterogeneity (Vdp), not oil viscosity - and is capped by injectivity: push resistance factor past RF* and the tight matrix plugs, injectivity collapses, oil falls. High TDS degrades the achievable viscosity, so an ATBS-grade polymer is assumed. The optimum concentration is set by injectivity in tight rock, not by chemical cost.

Polymer concentration → back-production → de-rate

Injected conc (C_inj, flat) Back-produced conc (C_back)
PW-treating Separation Dehydration
Only the polymer slug fraction of injected water carries polymer; the rest of the field injection is plain water. Back-produced concentration = C_inj × slug × (1 − retention) × breakthrough(year). Each unit's de-rate ramps with C_back up to its cap - so the whole surface constraint story is downstream of one number you set at the injectors. Raise C_inj and watch de-rate, chemical cost and the binding link all move together.

That chain - constraint → equipment → cost code → economics - is the decision. When the binding link migrates from the oil side to the water side under polymer, the dominant CAPEX migrates with it.

Assumptions & Limitations

What this demonstrator is for

It shows how polymer flooding can change the constraint profile of a mature light-oil carbonate development - from reservoir sweep and injectivity, through the surface facilities, to screening economics. The goal is mechanism understanding and option screening, not a production forecast.

It can help answer

  • Where does the next constraint appear once EOR adds oil and liquid?
  • Does improved sweep create a surface-facility problem?
  • How does injectivity (RF vs RF*) limit the achievable polymer response?
  • When does added recovery become limited by facilities rather than reservoir?
  • Which assumptions move the screening result the most?

Numerical values are calibrated to published analogues (see calibration sources), not universal industry constants.

Limitations. This is an engineering screening demonstrator, not a reservoir simulator or field development model. It does not perform history matching, compositional or sector/full-field simulation, detailed PVT, well-by-well modelling, polymer-degradation kinetics, geomechanics, facilities hydraulic simulation, or project-grade cost estimation. Facility de-rates, power, CAPEX, OPEX and economics use simplified screening correlations. Results support mechanism understanding, option screening and engineering discussion - not reserves booking, sanction decisions, or detailed design.
