Well-Pad & Corridor Siting Method

Built an integrated method for choosing well-pad locations and infrastructure corridors that cut lifecycle cost by 10–50%.

ClientSalym Petroleum Development (Shell JV)
Period2018–2023
RoleSenior Concept Engineer
GateDecision Review Board
10–50%10–50% lifecycle cost reduction depending on the case, with corridor length down 15–35% and earthworks down 10–30%. Adopted as company best practice for well-pad planning.

Context

Well-pad locations had historically been set mainly by the well pattern. That worked for simple structures, but with horizontal drilling and more complex topography it was pushing unit technical cost up and development efficiency down.

Role

Senior Concept Engineer: led a cross-discipline group across geology, drilling, infrastructure, economics and legal, and designed the evaluation framework.

Challenge

Hit drilling and geology targets at minimum lifecycle cost, within environmental and land-allocation limits, while balancing technical achievability against economics under high input-data uncertainty.

Action

  • Set up a group across geology, drilling, infrastructure, economics and legal.
  • Built an evaluation framework with more than 20 parameters: horizontal section length, entry angle, logistics, crossings, land-allocation limits, CAPEX and OPEX.
  • Generated 9 to 30 alternative pad locations per case, ranked them and shortlisted the top 3 to 5 with GIS models, cost models and scenario analysis.
  • Produced a recommended and an alternative option with quantified savings per driver and aligned them at the DRB.

Result

10–50% lifecycle cost reduction depending on the case; corridor length down 15–35% and earthworks down 10–30%. The method became company best practice for well-pad planning, with faster alignment between technical and economic teams and more predictable budgets.
